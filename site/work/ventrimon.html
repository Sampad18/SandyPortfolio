VentriMon — a non-invasive, continuous ICP monitoring device

A multidisciplinary concept built with two engineering students and a medical student during the Digital Medical Innovation Programme.

The Problem

Current ICP monitoring is invasive — and risky

Intracranial pressure (ICP) monitoring helps clinicians determine if abnormal cerebrospinal fluid pressure is causing a patient’s symptoms. Existing techniques, invasive and non-invasive alike, can lead to serious post-operative complications.

The Brief

Design goals & stakeholders

To innovate an affordable, safer, and continuous way to monitor ICP at tertiary care centers — preventing high infection risk and seizures for traumatic brain injury patients, comatose patients with abnormal head CT scans, and patients recovering from mass evacuation surgery.

Accurate — readings within ±3mm Hg of the gold standard
Reusable — durable materials for repeat use
Reliable — continuous, uninterrupted readings
Affordable — viable for tertiary care budgets

Research & Biomimicry

From gecko feet to a firmer grip

Mind-mapping, empathy mapping, clustering, user personas, and stakeholder mapping helped narrow down the design intervention best suited to patients. A gecko-inspired synthetic adhesive — where 1cm of tape can support 4kg of weight — became the biomimicry foundation for a headband that holds a transcranial doppler firmly in place without adhesives or straps.

The Concept

Concept VentriMon

A headband made of polycarbonates and thermoplastic elastomers for longevity and reuse, with a rubber inner grip to hold the transcranial doppler in place, gecko-inspired synthetic setae for firm grip over the temporal lobe, and a foam and cloth pad for comfortable, low-pressure wear.
